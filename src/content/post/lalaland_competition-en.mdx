My experience at La La Land 2024.

My last competition was in Tianjin five years ago.

## Set Out

I headed to the venue at 10:30 in the morning to check in (a Hilton, of course; the $70 registration fee was too expensive).

## Comp Process

This was a 24-hour competition, which means it ran nonstop for 24 hours. If you compete in many events (like me), you have to stay up all night. In fact, I did stay up all night.

Respect to the air conditioning here; I will never wear shorts to a 24h comp again...

Shivering all over in the middle of the night, I had to go to the bathroom after every solve to warm my hands with hot water and stay in good condition.

<img src="https://cdn.lyt0112.com/posts/lalaland-competition/images/0fe466b39d35/b5f69e8ce00a7d532bb54aeb5d6914bc.jpeg" alt="" style="width: 80%; height: auto; display: block; margin: 0 auto;" /><p style="text-align: center; font-style: italic; color: gray;"> Timetable </p>

I competed in nine events in total; here are two of the more interesting ones.

### 3x3

Cubing for 7 years: improved by 5 seconds in the first 3 years, and by another 5 seconds in the last 4 years...

At this competition I broke my single and average personal records, with a sub-10 single. In regular practice I get maybe one sub-10 out of 20 attempts, so I'm a bit excited.

<img src="https://cdn.lyt0112.com/posts/lalaland-competition/images/02f41b9eed00/a55df8e1a1f50489065e8d2daa64559b.jpeg" alt="" style="width: 50%; height: auto; display: block; margin: 0 auto;" /><p style="text-align: center; font-style: italic; color: gray;"> SUB 10 </p>

Next time, I'm going for a SUB 10 average!

### Pyraminx

I don't know why I often rank relatively high with plain L4E. This time I felt good in the second round, broke my personal records for single and average, and also made it to the final.

<img src="https://cdn.lyt0112.com/posts/lalaland-competition/images/6b43108ca431/27cde2edbc4f4f23d7712b35a5950f8d.jpeg" alt="" style="width: 50%; height: auto; display: block; margin: 0 auto;" /><p style="text-align: center; font-style: italic; color: gray;"> Pyraminx Final </p>

Maybe because I was too tired from staying up all night, I didn't perform well in the final, but I'm still very happy to have made it there.

### Differences Between China and US Competitions

At first, I thought US competitions ran the same way as Chinese ones, but there are actually quite a few differences.

In Chinese competitions, the judge takes your cube to the scrambling area, has a scrambler scramble it, and then brings it back to the same competition table. Competitors do not leave their seats until all five attempts are finished.

In US competitions, you first submit your cube. After it is scrambled, a Runner places your cube on an idle competition table that has a judge and calls your name. You go to this table to do the solve, and the judge records your result. After finishing one solve, you need to leave the competition table and return to the waiting area. The Runner will take your cube back to the scrambling area to wait for the next time your name is called at another competition table. So, you need to complete five solves on five different competition tables.

<img src="https://cdn.lyt0112.com/posts/lalaland-competition/images/87e5163a9d59/79c8be15c1efa460499d1df6665c0055.jpeg" alt="" style="width: 50%; height: auto; display: block; margin: 0 auto;" /><p style="text-align: center; font-style: italic; color: gray;"> Submitting cubes </p>

The US approach is more efficient because no table sits idle, and competitors waiting for scrambles do not occupy tables.

Judges are also organized differently. In the US, the judges are all competitors. Each person is assigned to a competing group and also to a judging group. When your judging group is called, you need to find a free judge seat and judge.

<img src="https://cdn.lyt0112.com/posts/lalaland-competition/images/63a66431ad52/706d865a79f21113a14b457eac5bdc7a.jpeg" alt="" style="width: 50%; height: auto; display: block; margin: 0 auto;" /><p style="text-align: center; font-style: italic; color: gray;"> I'm a Judge </p>

Of course, judging is oversubscribed: in one event you only do 5 solves yourself, but as a judge you have to judge everyone who comes to your table, about 50 solves, so you can also choose to slack off and skip judging when it's your turn.

However, if many people skip judging, there are not enough judges. The WCA Delegate then has to shout into the microphone to find people to judge, especially late at night when many kids have gone to bed, and sometimes there's only one person acting as a judge.

<img src="https://cdn.lyt0112.com/posts/lalaland-competition/images/3d544e58c05e/a75cce188fa719394bffe39a94a659af.jpeg" alt="" style="width: 50%; height: auto; display: block; margin: 0 auto;" /><p style="text-align: center; font-style: italic; color: gray;"> The judge's stopwatch ⏱️ </p>

In China, by contrast, judges sign up through questionnaires. Before the competition, organizers recruit judges by posting questionnaires in various chat groups. This ensures that there are enough judges, but the downside is that recruiting them takes effort.

## People & Friends

[Preston Hoing (2019HOIN01)](https://www.worldcubeassociation.org/persons/2019HOIN01) was the first person I met at the competition venue. He is a bit of a chatterbox and chatted with me very enthusiastically, even telling me about a great Clock scramble he got last time.

He competed in every event at this competition. The 24-hour all-event run was really exhausting for him, but he still persevered until the end, got his first 5BLD success, and even made it to the 3BLD final. Impressive. However, I forgot to take a photo with him, which is a bit regrettable.
