Nonstandard F2L covers cases with the white cross solved, FR as the target slot, and at least one target piece in another slot. The cases fall into six classes by where the corner and edge sit. A piece on top is first brought to a fixed position with a U turn, and cases that mirror each other across the FR–BL diagonal plane are merged, leaving 67 families.

Most algorithms come from J Perm's Advanced F2L[^jperm], 湛蓝's collection of nonstandard cases[^zhanlan], and BirdF2L[^birdf2l].

## Recognition and navigation

Positions are named by the faces they touch: corner positions use three letters, such as UFR and DFR, and edge positions use two, such as UR and FR. A slot is a bottom corner position together with the middle-layer edge position above it: FR = DFR + FR, FL = DFL + FL, BR = DBR + BR, BL = DBL + BL, so each slot shares its edge position's name. The target is the FR slot: the white-blue-red corner belongs at DFR and the blue-red edge at FR.

The mirror reflects the cube across the vertical diagonal plane through the FR and BL edge positions. F swaps with R and L swaps with B, along with their sticker colors, and outer turns reverse direction: `R U R'` becomes `F' U' F`. The slices `S` and `M` swap and keep their exponent. Open Mirror to see the diagram and algorithm for the other side.

<TutorialImages images={[{src:'https://cdn.lyt0112.com/posts/f2l-nonstandard/images/e1aa2ca196ae/notation.svg',alt:'U D F B R L · FR',width:1000,height:440}]} maxWidth="100%" />

The positions of the two target pieces select the class; their orientations select the case within it. When both pieces are on U or in their own target positions, the case is standard F2L, including a twisted corner at DFR or a flipped edge at FR.

## B. Corner in another slot, edge on top

The corner is at DFL, DBR, or DBL and the edge is on top. The catalog puts the edge at UR; from UF, UB, or UL, first turn `U'`, `U`, or `U2`, which leaves the corner in place. Mirror diagrams also keep the edge at UR, with the needed U turn built into each mirror algorithm.

Their mirrors put the corner at DBR. ## Five ideas

Classes A–F sort cases by where the pieces start. The techniques below work on how the pieces relate, so they cut across the classes and apply to standard F2L as well.

### Let the extraction set up a familiar insertion

In a wrong-slot case, extraction, adjustment, and insertion are best planned together: a familiar extraction can leave the pieces exactly where the final insertion starts. Macky runs the first half of a familiar case at the wrong slot, leaving an easy top-layer case for the real slot.[^macky] That case only needs to be familiar relative to the actual slot, so no rotation is spent matching a standard diagram.[^speedcubevault]

In A08, `F U2 F'` brings the FL edge to UB with blue up, while the corner leaves UFR and returns with white still facing right. This is the basic case: `R` joins the pair and `U R'` inserts it.

### Pick the route by edge orientation

Relative to the current grip, the blue-red edge is oriented when blue faces U on top, or F or B in the middle layer. An oriented edge can be solved with R, U, and L alone.[^orientation]

A05's edge sits in BR with blue back, so it is oriented and `(R' U' R) (R U R')` stays in R and U; the two adjacent `R` turns merge into `R2`, giving `R' U' R2 U R'`. A08's edge sits in FL with blue left, so it is unoriented: the `F` that lifts it out flips it, and `U2` moves it clear of `F'`, which leads to the same insertion.

### Carry a joined pair as one block

A pair already joined in a wrong slot can move as a unit. Pranav Prabhu's `R' F U' F' R` transfers a joined pair from FL directly into BR, with no cube rotation in between.[^pranav]

E07 moves a joined pair from BL into FR. In `R (L U2 L') R'`, `R` opens the target slot, `L U2 L'` carries the block to UFR and UR, and `R'` brings it into FR with the pair still joined.

### Keyhole: borrow an unsolved position

Keyhole applies when one piece of the pair is already solved. D changes which bottom corner position sits under each middle edge, so an unsolved slot's corner or edge position becomes a temporary insertion site; the missing piece goes in there and D is undone. Pseudoslotting uses the same offset across two slots: D aligns the missing corner and edge positions of two slots, a corner and an edge from different original pairs go in together, and undoing D completes both.[^keyhole]

K01, a standard case, shows the solved-corner variant. The corner is solved at DFR and the edge is at UF with blue up. The FL corner, outlined in purple, must be unsolved because it temporarily takes the target corner's place. `D` moves the target corner to DBR and the FL corner to DFR, `R U' R'` inserts the edge, and `D'` restores the target corner.

### Choose the current solution for the next pair

Multislotting changes how the current pair is solved so that it also pairs up or simplifies the next one. In one of Macky's examples, both solutions start with `L U L'`; after that, the longer insertion `U R U2 R'` replaces `R U' R'` because it also forms the next pair.[^macky]

Empty slots usually allow several solutions. Jayden McNeill weighs rotations, which slot ends up filled, next-pair visibility, move count, and fingertricks, criteria that often conflict.[^jayden] A01 has a six-move RU route, `U' R' U R2 U' R'`, and a five-move RF route, `R F R2 F' R'`; pick the one whose turn transitions and fingertricks run more smoothly in your hands.
