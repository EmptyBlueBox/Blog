Square-1 has eight corners spanning 60° each and eight edges spanning 30° each. Slice turns move pieces between the two layers, changing both their piece counts and the puzzle's shape. This tutorial follows Basic Lars: restore cubeshape, separate the top and bottom colors, then permute the pieces.

## Notation and diagrams

Hold the puzzle with the short section of the middle layer at the front left. Keep your left hand on that half. U is the upper layer and D the lower layer. In each pair of diagrams, U is on the left and D on the right. **Both are viewed from above**, as though you could see through the upper layer. Each layer diagram marks the front with `F ↓` and the middle-layer slice direction with a dashed line.

| Notation | Action |
| --- | --- |
| `(a,b)` | Turn U by a units of 30° and D by b units of 30° |
| Positive / negative | Positive U is clockwise from above. Positive D is clockwise from below, so it appears counterclockwise in these D diagrams |
| `/` | Turn the right half through 180°; both layers must line up with the middle-layer cut |
| `U`, `D` | `(3,0)`, `(0,3)`: a 90° turn of that layer |
| `'`, `2` | Reverse direction, or turn twice: `U' = (-3,0)` and `U2 = (6,0)` |
| `M2` | The sequence `(1,0) / (-1,-1) / (0,1)` in this tutorial |

You may adjust U and D to match a case before starting its algorithm; keep the grip fixed during execution.

## 1. Cubeshape: build a six-corner star

First collect **six of the eight corners** in the bottom layer, with no edges between them. This makes a six-corner star. The other two corners and all eight edges remain in the upper layer.

Keep the existing run of corners to the left of the bottom-layer cut, with one end against the cut. Turn U to place another corner in the right half so that a slice brings it beside the preserved run. After joining it, adjust D to keep the longer run on the left. Build a run of three corners and move it to the bottom left half. Preserve that half while using only U turns and right-half slices to collect three consecutive corners in the upper layer. Bring that group down with a slice to complete the star.

With the star below, count the edges in the two gaps between the upper layer's two corners. The five possibilities are 4+4, 3+5, 2+6, 1+7, and 0+8. Adjust U and D until the corners and cut lines match the relevant diagram, then execute its algorithm.

## 2. Separate the corner colors

Choose a top and bottom color; the diagrams use yellow above and white below. Move the four top-color corners into U and the four bottom-color corners into D. Their side colors will be arranged later.

Count the misplaced corners in each layer. For one per layer, adjust U and D to match the first diagram and use the single-corner exchange. For two per layer, place them together in the pictured half and use the second sequence. With more misplaced corners, exchange a group first, then recognize the remaining case.

## 3. Separate the edge colors

Move top-color edges into U and bottom-color edges into D while preserving the corner colors. When each layer has one misplaced edge, use the single-edge exchange. With two misplaced edges per layer, first try to align them with the double-edge case.
